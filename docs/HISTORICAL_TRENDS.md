# Historical trends

Long-term opportunities and threats, and the rules that keep them honest.

Governed by MORPHEUS_CHARACTER.md §6 and KNOWLEDGE_POLICY.md §2.

---

## 1. The finding that makes this necessary

NeoOS already has a Capital Radar. It shows **what materially changed since the
last report**. That test is the right one for a daily briefing and it is
structurally blind to the thing this product exists to answer.

A variable that moves 0.3% a month never wins a "what changed today" comparison.
Over twenty years it decides the outcome. The radar filters out precisely the
trends that dominate a generational objective, and it does so silently, because
nothing about the mechanism looks wrong.

> **A daily briefing and a generational trend run on different clocks. Mixing
> them makes the slow thing invisible.**

So trends are a separate layer with a separate cadence, not another input to the
daily comparison. The two answers stay two answers (PRODUCT_DEFINITION.md §2).
Trends inform them; they do not become a third.

## 2. Three things, often confused

| | States | Example | Permitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trend** | What has happened, measured | "Equity share went 40% → 68% over 18 months" | Yes |
| **Projection** | What happens if it continues, assumption named | "At this rate it reaches 80% in a year" | Yes, labelled |
| **Forecast** | What will happen | "Equities will fall next year" | **Never** |

A projection is a trend plus a stated assumption, and the assumption is shown
every time it is used. NeoOS does not forecast, and does not imply one by
drawing a line past the last observation.

## 3. When a series is long enough to be called anything

The most common way to lie with a trend is to fit one to too few points over too
short a window. Every observation therefore carries how much it rests on.

| Sufficiency | Requires | Says |
|---|---|---|
| `insufficient` | fewer than 3 points, or under 90 days | "Not yet a trend. Two points are a line." |
| `indicative` | 3+ points over 90+ days | "A direction, not an established pattern." |
| `established` | 6+ points over 2+ years | "A pattern in the record." |

**Even `established` is short for this objective.** Two years is one market mood.
The label describes the series, not the world, and the interface says so.

### The regime caveat

From KNOWLEDGE_POLICY.md §5: anything only true since 2009 has not been tested.
A series measured entirely inside one regime describes that regime. It is
reported with the window it covers, never as a property of the asset.

## 4. Threats and opportunities are not symmetric

MORPHEUS_CHARACTER.md §2: care, expressed as behaviour, is asymmetric attention
to ruin. That is a threshold, not a tone.

**A threat is flagged at half the magnitude of an opportunity.** A drift that
could end the plan deserves attention earlier than a drift that could improve
it. A system that treats a 5% gain and a 5% loss alike is optimising for return
on behalf of someone who asked for preservation.

## 5. Where trends can honestly come from

| Source | Status | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Declared position history** | **Built** | Drift: concentration, passive share, liquidity, net worth |
| **Structural conditions** | **Built** | Standing long-horizon risks, from a single profile |
| **Filed fundamentals** | Available, not yet wired | Multi-year revenue, margin, leverage, share count (dilution) |
| **Official statistics** | **No provider** | Purchasing power, rates, real yields |
| **Market price history** | **No provider** | Long-run valuation and drawdown history |
| **Knowledge precedent** | Needs the corpus | "This resembles X; what mattered then was Y" |

Two of these are honest gaps rather than work in progress.

**Macro series have no provider.** Without one, any claim about purchasing power
or interest-rate regime would be fabricated. Central banks and official
statistics agencies publish these directly and free, which is what the source
policy already prefers — primary, attributable, no aggregator in between. That
is a provider to build, not an assumption to make in the meantime.

**Precedent needs the corpus.** Until there are chosen works to reason from,
NeoOS has no basis for "this resembles the early 1970s" beyond model recall,
which KNOWLEDGE_POLICY.md §3 forbids asserting.

Until each exists, the corresponding trend is **absent and named as absent**. It
is not estimated, and it is not filled from general knowledge.

## 6. Structural conditions: threats that do not move

Not every long-term threat is a trend. Some are standing facts, visible from a
single profile, and they are often the largest.

- The entire position denominated in one currency.
- Every asset in one jurisdiction.
- Every asset behind one custodian.
- No succession structure, so distribution follows default law.
- Nobody who could take over the affairs.
- Support that never ends, funded by income that stops when the subject does.
- A majority of value that cannot be sold, or that rests on the subject's own
  estimate rather than any market.

These need no history at all, which means NeoOS can name the largest threats to
a position on the first day the position is declared. That is worth more than
any trend line, and it is available immediately.

## 7. What must never happen

- **A trend line extended past its last observation** without the assumption
  named and the word projection used.
- **A trend built across currencies** without a verified rate. Same rule as
  everywhere else: totals are per currency, or they are not totals.
- **A trend asserted from model recall.** If NeoOS did not measure it or cite
  it, it does not say it.
- **An opportunity reported without the corresponding threat having been
  looked for.** The disconfirmation requirement applies here too.
- **A slow threat omitted because nothing changed today.** That is the failure
  this whole layer exists to fix.

---

## 8. How to know whether it is working

1. Does a slow, large threat appear on a day when nothing changed?
2. Does every trend state how many points and how long a window it rests on?
3. Are threats surfaced at smaller magnitudes than opportunities?
4. Is a projection always labelled, with its assumption visible?
5. Are the missing sources named as missing, rather than quietly absent?

Question 1 is the real test. It is the one the radar cannot pass.
