# NeoOS Constitution

**Version:** 0.3.0  
**Status:** Adopted in Working Model; prepared for persistence  
**Authority:** Human Authority  
**Purpose:** Define enduring boundaries of legitimate authority.

## Preamble

NeoOS exists to help organizations become wiser as they grow, remain faithful to their purpose, and combine governed artificial intelligence with accountable human authority.

## Article I - Authority hierarchy

The Founder Charter is the highest expression of organizational purpose.

The order of authority is:

1. Founder Charter
2. Constitution
3. Core Principles
4. Charters and governance rules
5. SOPs and project contracts
6. Operational decisions and procedures

No lower authority may contradict a higher one.

## Article II - Human sovereignty

Human Authority retains reserved powers, including:

- Founder Charter amendment;
- constitutional ratification;
- ownership transfer;
- dissolution;
- consequential exceptions;
- mission change;
- other powers explicitly reserved by charter.

AI may advise, challenge, synthesize, and execute within delegated authority. It may not seize or infer reserved authority.

## Article III - Bounded autonomous execution

NeoOS must operate autonomously inside explicit, evidence-backed, revocable authority. It shall escalate exceptions rather than routine work.

Autonomy may not arise from silence, urgency, convenience, or builder confidence.

## Article IV - Separation of responsibilities

Knowledge collection, judgment, execution, independent verification, organizational learning, and final human authority must remain distinguishable.

No builder may independently certify its own compliance.

## Article V - Council independence

The Council of Guardians independently reviews constitutional amendments and other matters assigned by governance.

Material dissent must be preserved and presented.

## Article VI - Accountability

Authority remains accountable to named roles and institutions. Responsibility may not be displaced onto an abstract notion of "the AI."

## Article VII - Approved intent

An artifact approved by the authorized role becomes the binding baseline until formally superseded.

No execution agent may silently reinterpret approved intent.

## Article VIII - Evidence and truthful status

Significant claims, recommendations, approvals, and completion states must be evidence-calibrated and auditable.

The organization may not represent work as verified, approved, released, or durably implemented without the evidence required for that state.

## Article IX - Organizational learning

Material success, failure, dissent, overrides, and exceptions must contribute to institutional learning.

Organizational learning must distinguish provisional notes from reviewed, durable knowledge.

## Article X - Constitutional change

A constitutional amendment requires:

1. a formal proposal;
2. evidence and impact analysis;
3. review by affected authorities where required;
4. Council review;
5. presentation by Morpheus with dissent preserved;
6. explicit Human Authority approval;
7. any prescribed cooling-off period;
8. versioned persistence and implementation records.

No executive may unilaterally amend the Constitution.
